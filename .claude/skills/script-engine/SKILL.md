# Script Engine
## Role
Convert an approved, format-agnostic Concept into the generative portion
of a Creative Record.
Produce:
- execution choices;
- Hook Strategy;
- persuasion beats;
- complete word-for-word script;
- Brilliance language refinement;
- Chile localisation;
- hook copy, visual, and audio;
- storyboard / shot list;
- continuity-aware visual planning.
Execute the approved strategic argument completely.
Do not change what that argument means merely to improve execution.
Do not strengthen claims, invent proof, or leave production-facing
placeholders.
## Inputs
Reason only over prepared inputs supplied by orchestration, such as:
- Gate-2-approved Concept;
- source Hypothesis dimensions;
- approved Audience Model projection;
- approved Persuasion Projection;
- Product Truth projection;
- pinned claim-freedom tier;
- pinned permitted claim territories;
- supplied verified claim candidates;
- required qualifications;
- campaign offer when applicable;
- brand and negative constraints;
- assigned or available format profiles;
- narrative-pattern projections;
- available Style Profile projections;
- authorised asset projections;
- target duration;
- tool-capability projection;
- relevant Chile customer-language observations;
- voice constraints or voice-specification input;
- retrieved `KNW-schwartz-persuasion`;
- retrieved `KNW-schwartz-language` for the language pass.
Treat inputs as model-facing projections.
Do not retrieve arbitrary files.
Do not inspect quarantined knowledge.
Do not allocate canonical IDs.
Do not persist Creative Records.
Do not determine asset rights.
Do not determine whether a claim is VERIFIED.
Do not calculate claim freedom.
## Procedure
### 1. Preserve the approved Concept
Begin from the approved strategic argument.
Preserve:
- inherited Hypothesis dimensions;
- core message;
- promise;
- product role;
- reason to believe;
- belief bridge;
- evidence requirements;
- permitted claim territory.
Do not:
- invent a new angle;
- change the starting belief;
- create a new promise;
- strengthen the Reason to Believe;
- alter the strategic dimensions.
If effective execution requires changing strategy, request an upstream
revision instead.
### 2. Define Hook Strategy before final execution
Determine:
- entry awareness;
- entry device;
- belief entry point;
- rationale.
Hook Strategy answers where the creative should enter the audience's
current state.
It is not yet exact copy.
Do not let a preferred style redefine the strategic entry point.
### 3. Choose among supplied execution options
When orchestration has not already fixed them, reason among supplied
valid choices for:
- format;
- narrative pattern;
- style;
- target duration;
- pacing intent;
- language register;
- voice specification.
Use only supplied registry/profile options.
Respect explicit format assignments and campaign format mix.
Do not embed taxonomies or profile policy in this Skill.
Do not copy recognisable competitor creative, dialogue, claims, branding,
or signature execution.
### 4. Preserve the pinned evidence boundary
Treat supplied:
- claim-freedom tier;
- permitted territories;
- verified claim candidates;
- required qualifications;
- brand constraints;
- negative constraints;
as execution boundaries.
Decide how an allowed idea is expressed.
Do not decide that an unsupported territory becomes allowed.
Do not invent evidence because the script would sound stronger.
### 5. Build persuasion beats
Use the approved Concept and `KNW-schwartz-persuasion` to sequence the
argument.
Beat functions may include:
- HOOK;
- PROBLEM;
- AGITATION;
- MECHANISM;
- PROOF;
- OBJECTION;
- BENEFIT;
- CTA.
Each beat must have a clear communication purpose.
Schwartz may guide:
- sequencing;
- proof placement;
- momentum;
- mechanism explanation;
- objection handling.
It remains subordinate to Product Truth, evidence, approved strategy,
current market research, real customer language, brand constraints,
and compliance boundaries.
### 6. Write the complete strategic draft
Write the entire script word for word in clear neutral strategic Spanish
before localisation.
Every spoken line must contain actual copy.
Every on-screen-text line must contain actual copy.
Never emit placeholders such as:
- `[creator explains benefit]`;
- `<insert proof>`;
- `TBD`;
- `XXX`;
- `...`.
Represent non-verbal actions structurally rather than disguising
unwritten content as copy.
### 7. Keep the script addressable
Structure the script with:
- speakers;
- beats;
- ordered segments;
- speaker assignment;
- segment kind;
- exact text;
- on-screen text;
- performance direction;
- CTA.
Follow the supplied format profile.
For example, a supplied profile may require:
- speaker turn order;
- dialogue;
- narration;
- reactions;
- interruptions;
- visual-only beats.
Do not modify the format profile.
### 8. Run the Brilliance language pass
Use `KNW-schwartz-language` only in the dedicated language-refinement
pass.
Improve:
- first-pass comprehension;
- concreteness;
- relationship clarity;
- transitions;
- rhythm;
- spoken flow;
- caption readability;
- visual translatability.
The pass may improve expression.
It may not change:
- product facts;
- claim scope;
- qualifications;
- core promise;
- belief bridge;
- approved strategic meaning.
Surface ambiguity that may imply more than the literal wording states.
### 9. Localise to Chilean Spanish
After the neutral strategic draft and language pass, perform one distinct
Chile localisation and spoken-naturalness pass.
Target:
- natural conversational `es-CL`;
- believable rhythm;
- appropriate register;
- consistency with supplied real customer language;
- speakability.
Naturalness is more important than forced slang.
Do not:
- add slang merely to signal Chile;
- stereotype Chilean speakers;
- invent customer expressions;
- strengthen claims;
- alter required qualification wording when exact wording is required.
Localisation changes expression, not meaning.
### 10. Execute the Hook
Produce the four addressable hook parts:
1. strategy;
2. copy;
3. visual;
4. audio.
The exact hook copy must also appear as the corresponding script segment.
Hook execution may specify:
- exact words;
- on-screen text;
- first visual beat;
- framing;
- subject;
- product presence;
- delivery;
- SFX;
- music character;
- first-words timing intent.
Do not create a standalone Hook ID.
### 11. Translate the script into a visual plan
Create a generator-usable storyboard / shot list.
For each scene, specify useful production-facing information such as:
- order and timing;
- subject;
- concrete action;
- framing;
- camera movement;
- linked dialogue segments;
- product presence;
- on-screen text;
- performance direction;
- lighting intent;
- transition;
- audio intent;
- continuity references.
Do not add film-production metadata that downstream generation does not
need merely to make the plan appear professional.
### 12. Plan generation units and continuity
When the supplied tool-capability projection requires segmentation,
group scenes into generation units.
Reason about continuity for:
- character;
- wardrobe;
- product;
- location;
- lighting;
- voice;
- style.
Describe stable continuity anchors across seams.
Use the supplied duration capability.
Do not hardcode a vendor generation-duration limit.
Do not decide the deterministic feasibility result.
### 13. Preserve qualification placement
When exact qualifications are supplied, place them in the relevant
script segments.
Do not:
- omit them for pacing;
- weaken them;
- hide them;
- silently rewrite them.
Deterministic services later verify final qualification presence.
### 14. Surface execution risk
Before handoff, identify:
- possible implied claims;
- ambiguous comparative wording;
- possible claim intensification;
- unclear causal relationships;
- wording that cannot be localised naturally without changing meaning;
- execution requiring unavailable evidence;
- visuals that may communicate more than the spoken copy;
- unresolved production-feasibility concerns.
Do not self-authorise questionable language.

### Strategy projection

### Execution

- pacing intent;
- language;

### Hook
- strategy;
- exact copy;
- visual;

### Script
- speakers;
- persuasion beats;
- ordered segments;
- exact spoken copy;
- exact on-screen text;
- CTA;
- performance direction.
### Language findings
- ambiguity;
- comprehension issues;
- naturalness issues;
- visual-translatability issues.
### Chile localisation

### Visual plan

- scene-to-script relationships;
- generation-unit proposal;
- continuity anchors;
- production-facing direction.
### Execution gaps
- evidence dependency;
- feasibility concern;
- ambiguity;
- upstream revision need.
Do not return or decide:
- a new Hypothesis;
- a new Concept;
- canonical Creative ID;
- canonical version;
- hashes;
- persisted Creative Record;
- claim verification;
- deterministic claim bindings;
- localisation-invariance result;
- deterministic hard compliance result;
- critic verdict;
- deterministic feasibility result;
- experiment lock result;
- Creative state;
- Production Package;
- production-tool routing.
## Handoffs
After this Skill returns:
1. orchestration validates the model-facing Creative proposal;
2. deterministic format/schema checks run;
3. claim-to-evidence binding runs on final localised text;
4. required qualifications are checked;
5. localisation claim invariance is checked;
6. deterministic deny-list and safety validation run;
7. deterministic feasibility is derived;
8. Creative Critic reviews in separate context;
9. bounded revision may return to this Skill;
10. `compliance-reviewer` performs compliance judgement;
11. locking and Gate 3 remain outside this Skill.
The Skill never persists the Creative itself.
## Failure & Uncertainty
Surface a failure or revision need when:
- the Concept cannot be executed without changing meaning;
- no supplied format is workable;
- a required claim lacks usable supplied evidence;
- an exact qualification conflicts with the intended execution;
- requested duration cannot plausibly hold the required argument;
- localisation would change claim meaning;
- Chile customer-language evidence is too weak for a strongly local register;
- style constraints conflict with the argument;
- requested execution exceeds supplied tool capability;
- continuity cannot be described coherently;
- visuals would imply a stronger claim than the script;
- wording remains materially ambiguous.
Valid conservative outcomes include:
- execution revision request;
- evidence request;
- format-fit failure;
- ambiguity finding;
- upstream Concept revision request;
- feasibility concern.
Do not repair upstream defects silently.
